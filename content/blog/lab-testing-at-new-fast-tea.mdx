Every claim on this site is attached to a batch number, because that is the
only unit a laboratory can actually test. A brand cannot be tested. A batch
can.

## What accreditation means

A laboratory accredited by NABL to ISO/IEC 17025:2017 has been assessed on
its methods, its equipment calibration, and its competence to run the
specific tests listed on its certificate. The certificate has a scope and an
expiry date, and both are worth checking — ours are printed next to every
report we publish.

Accreditation does not mean the laboratory checked everything. It means the
things it did check were checked properly.

## What a panel covers, and what it doesn't

A test panel is a list. Seven dyes on a report means seven dyes were looked
for. It says nothing about an eighth.

This is the single most common way a clean-looking report misleads people,
so on this site an untested parameter is rendered as its own state — never
folded in with the passes:

    method="HPLC"

  />
  <LabResult
    analyte="Measured, and under the limit"
    method="HPLC"
    result="0.42 mg/kg"
    limit="10 mg/kg"
    state="within-limit"
    note="An illustrative value, not a result from any batch."
  />
  <LabResult
    analyte="Not on the panel"
    method="—"
    state="not-tested"
    note="No sample was analysed for this."
  />
</LabTable>

The middle row matters as much as the first. “Within limit” is a real
measurement of something that is present in a permitted amount — it is not
the same claim as “not detected”, and we do not present them as though they
were.

<Disclosure title="What we cannot promise">

A report covers the sample that reached the laboratory, on the date it was
received. It is evidence about that batch. It is not a guarantee about a
batch packed six months later, which is why we test them separately and
publish each one.

</Disclosure>

## Reading a report yourself

Three things worth checking on any laboratory report, ours included:

1. **The batch and the dates.** Does the batch on the report match the batch
   on the packet in your hand? Is the analysis date after the concern that
   prompted it?
2. **The parameter list.** What was actually tested, and what is missing?
3. **The accreditation.** Which body, which certificate number, and is it
   still valid?
